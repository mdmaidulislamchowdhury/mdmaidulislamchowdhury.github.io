About

I am a PhD candidate in the Department of Economics at the University of Calgary. My research focuses on applied macroeconomics, particularly monetary economics. I study how alternative measures of money affect key questions in monetary economics, including money demand, money velocity, and business-cycle fluctuations.

I hold an MA in Economics from the University of South Carolina. Before beginning my doctoral studies, I worked in the Research Department of the Bangladesh Bank, where I conducted research on monetary policy, financial markets, and the Bangladesh economy.

JMP

Stochastic Trends, Economic Fluctuations, and Classical Money Demand Functions: The Evidence from the Different Types of Money

Abstract

This paper examines whether empirical support for the monetary extension of the balanced-growth framework depends on how money is measured. I study long-run relationships among real output, consumption, investment, real money balances, and the user cost of money using simple-sum, original Divisia, credit-augmented Divisia, and credit-augmented Divisia inside monetary aggregates. Each aggregate is paired with its corresponding aggregate-specific user cost to maintain consistency with aggregation theory. Using U.S. quarterly data through 2026:q2, I apply the Johansen maximum likelihood procedure and test the long-run restrictions implied by balanced-growth theory. The results provide weak support for the balanced-growth restrictions at different sample lengths subject to availability. In the common sample, none of the simple-sum or original Divisia measures supports the money demand restriction in the three-variable system, while only Divisia M4 aggregate supports it in the five-variable system. By contrast, credit-augmented Divisia measures provide more frequent support for the money demand restriction, particularly in the five-variable system. The consumption-output and investment-output restrictions remain unsupported. These findings show that improved monetary measurement can strengthen empirical support for the theoretical money demand relationship without establishing the full set of balanced-growth restrictions.

(with Apostolos Serletis)
